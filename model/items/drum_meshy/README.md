# War drum from a generated model (Meshy), two variants

Source: a drum with a leather strap generated by the user in Meshy AI (`drum_meshy.glb`, 56 MB, **not in the repo**: kept in `models/` outside it, https://drive.google.com/file/d/1F37Rb2s-uLK0jlD_M9JR2nTFaztX8Txs/view; record the licence of the generator's output). The file is one mesh of 574k vertices and 1378 loose pieces.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Drum_meshy_sash.vd` | the drum alone (strap removed) laid over the ORIGINAL body sash of the client (anim 490), 35 actions x 5 directions |
| `Drum_meshy_strap.vd` | the drum with ITS OWN strap bent over the neck and the nape, no body sash |

Drum (the clamps and rods stick out ~2 cm past the hoops), heads to the sides, the badge on the shell looks forward, rigid on `chest`, the torso hides what is behind it (back views show only the sash / the strap). The model is 0.97 of its width deep: squeezed along its axis so that it takes less room across the body.
- **A (with the sash):** 37.6 cm across the hoops, 26 cm deep (squeeze x0.69), centre x +0.03 m (on the middle of the ORIGINAL body in the front view: at -0.02 it sat 1.5 px to the left of it), z 1.224 m (2 px lower than the first version's 1.28 m, 2 px wider: the user's wish after looking at the sprites). Renders put together per action (`compose_layers.py --alt`, each recipe in this folder):

Measured on the 578 frames (`model/items/drum/README.md`), the legs 10.4 %, the arms and hands 27 % of the frames at the first placement.
- **B (own strap):** 28 cm, 23 cm deep (x0.76), centre (-0.02, -0.32, 1.325) m, as in `model/items/drum/README.md`.

Contact in the side view (direction 2), all 210 frames: besides the walks, the frames in which the drum still hung more than 1.6 px in front of the original body (spell directed 3-5, spell area 4-6, mounted 1H attack 0-2, mounted 2H attack 1, 1H slash 2, 1H bash 6, die forward 0 and 5) are moved towards the body by `fit_item_contact.py --only-above 1.6 --cap 2.0` (at most 2 px = 5.6 cm; the frames below 1.6 px stay as they were; the shifts are in `item_motion.npz` of the `*_motion.json` item, its `--parent chest` run). Result: the mean gap (daylight between the drum and the body over the rows of the drum) is 0.0-1.8 px in all visible frames, 207 of 210 touch the body in at least one row (3 frames the drum is hidden behind the body or the arms: attack 2h slash 2, crossbow 6, block 2); the last frame of the death on the belly (22) lies beside the body on purpose. The mean gap is not zero because the drum is round and the torso is flat: they touch at the chest ridge.

The membrane: the texture baked from the model's high-poly original had black smudges on both heads (rays of the bake hitting the inside of the hoop); `uo_fix_texture.py` (recipe `"heal_heads": true`) finds the dark pixels on the head islands (darker than 0.62 of the median of the membrane) and fills them with the colour of the surrounding membrane and its grain taken from random healthy pixels, not with one flat colour (151 of 498 head pixels).

Quality (all recipes here): 100k vertices (was 30k), texture baked at 4096 px (was 2048; the sprite still gets the texture averaged to its resolution), `"scene": {"uo_samples": 2048}` (default 512, adaptive sampling with the strict threshold): the full 35-action render takes ~25 min on 4 cores, the partial ones 2-5 min.

Rebuild (each render ~15 min):
1. `python pipeline/split_drum_model.py models/drum_meshy.glb models/split` (B: `--dia 0.28 --depth 0.23`): the axis of the drum from a circle fit of its front head, the pieces whose farthest vertex is more than 1.2 x that radius from the axis are the strap and its hardware (160k of 574k vertices); both parts centred on the drum, scaled (`--pct 92`: the outer edge of the hoops is the radius), textures kept.
2. A: `python pipeline/uo_make_item.py model/items/drum_meshy/drum_only.json --out OUT --vd` (all actions); every other recipe of the table: `uo_make_item.py RECIPE --out DIR` (builds the item, 25 s), then `run_render_headless.py DIR/item.blend DIR/render 'LAYER="clothing"' "ONLY=['00_walk_unarmed', ...]" "CANVAS=(256,256)" "ANCHOR=(128,192)"` for the actions of the table. The death: `uo_make_item.py drum_only_death.json --out DD`, `python pipeline/drum_death_motion.py DD/item.blend` (writes `DD/item_motion.npz`, the motion that `render_uo_layer.py` `motion_fix` applies to an item with the property `uo_motion`), then render `22_die_backward` as above. The walks: `uo_make_item.py drum_only_pelvis_motion.json --out WM`, render `00_walk_unarmed` and `01_walk_armed` once as they are (into `W0/render`, or take them from the `drum_only_pelvis.json` render), `python pipeline/fit_item_contact.py WM/item.blend W0/render --action 00_walk_unarmed --action 01_walk_armed` (writes `WM/item_motion.npz`), render the two actions of `WM/item.blend` again. The sash from the client (see `model/items/drum/README.md`) and `python pipeline/compose_layers.py anim_0490.vd OUT/render OUT/Drum_meshy_sash.vd --alt 0,1=WM/render --alt 2,3=PELVIS/render --alt 4,5,17=CLOSE/render --alt 6=PELVIS0/render --alt 26,31=FWD3/render --alt 32=FWD45/render --alt 22=DD/render`. The split: `split_drum_model.py ... models/split376 --dia 0.376 --depth 0.26`.
3. B: `python pipeline/uo_make_item.py model/items/drum_meshy/drum_strap.json --out OUT --vd` (`uo_fit_strap.py`: the centre line of the strap is found, laid over the skin of the shoulders and the nape through `WAYPOINTS`, the strap bent along it; it keeps its stitching and hardware, 3.6 cm -> 6.5 cm wide (`WIDEN` 1.8: a 3.6 cm strap is 1.3 px and vanishes), stretched x1.25 along its length to fit the path (0.77 m -> 0.97 m); the clothing layer gets `uo_edge_floor` 24 so that the dark leather keeps a rim).

`item_qa.py`: A inside the body 1.5 % of the vertices (deepest 109 mm, worst: die forward; the base render); B 6.9 % (the strap rests on the neck, which moves with the head while the strap is rigid on `chest`; deepest 70 mm). The texture is baked (2048 px for the drum, 1024 for the strap) from the original and averaged to the sprite's resolution; the shell comes out redder than in the model's own render (UO light on a saturated orange wood).
Not checked: the horse in the mounted actions, the strap against the head in the head-turning actions (it would need weights on the neck), the game itself.
